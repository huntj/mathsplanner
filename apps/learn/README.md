# @mathsplanner/learn

Learn Maths, the pupils' app: a Duolingo-style path through the years with Dot, the mascot. See the [proposal](../../docs/proposals/learn-app.md) for the whole plan.

The course, the lessons, the review schedule and the pupil's progress are in [`@mathsplanner/learning`](../../packages/learning/README.md), which Maths City will share: files named below without a folder (`course.ts`, `lesson.ts`, `progress.ts` and so on) are in `packages/learning/src`. The screens that play a lesson (`LessonView.tsx`, `UnitLessonView.tsx`, `unit-screens.tsx`, `Picture.tsx` and their parts) are in [`@mathsplanner/lesson-player`](../../packages/lesson-player/README.md). What is in `src` here is the app itself: the welcome, the path, the skill sheet and the Practise and You pages.

A first visit has Dot say hello, then asks for the pupil's year and a daily goal. The path shows the year's units from `data/course.json`, each in its topic's colour, with its skills as a winding line of nodes and a unit review at the end. A ring round each node shows the levels done, and each skill's levels come from the depths its generator supports (`skillLevels` in `course.ts`). Finished skills are coloured, mastered ones gold with a tick and legendary ones gold with a star; the first unfinished skill is the current one, with Dot beside it, and the skills after it are locked. Tapping a node opens a sheet with the skill, its levels and what each asks, and starts its next lesson.

A lesson (`lesson.ts`, played by `LessonView.tsx`) is eight questions: six on the skill at the level being worked on, easiest first, and two review questions from skills already finished in the year. A brand-new skill opens on a worked example. Answers use the shared widgets from `@mathsplanner/ui` and are marked by `@mathsplanner/marking`; Check slides up a panel with a cheer, or the misconception behind a wrong answer, the right answer and the worked solution on request ("Show me how"). "Show me" gives the answer to a pupil who is stuck. A question answered wrongly comes back at the end with new numbers, at most twice. The end screen shows XP (10, or 15 for a perfect lesson), the questions right first time, and today's lessons or the streak, and says what the lesson did for the skill; the skill goes up a level and the misconceptions shown are kept for later "fix my mistakes" practice. The pupil's page shows their streak, XP and skills finished and mastered, and holds their preferences (daily goal, colours, sounds and challenge mode). **See an example**, on the welcome and the pupil's page, fills in example progress about halfway through Year 7.

Practice keeps skills fresh (`memory.ts`, `practice.ts`). A skill finished on the path goes into a review schedule (a Leitner system of six boxes, due back after 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 and 60 days): right when due, it moves up a box; wrong, it moves down two and comes back tomorrow. A due skill fades on the path, and its sheet offers "Refresh this skill". The Practise tab has a review of what is due (or extra practice on the stalest skills when nothing is), "Fix my mistakes" (questions chosen because they offer the misconceptions the pupil has shown, which count down as they are put right), the unit reviews waiting, and a bar of how well the finished skills are remembered. Each unit ends in a review, and the path in a year review, each worth bonus XP the first time.

Motivation, gently (`rewards.ts`): XP, a daily goal and a streak; a streak freeze for every seven days of streak (two at most), spent without asking when a day is missed; sixteen badges for milestones, shown on the end screen when earned and collected on the pupil's page; challenge mode, three hearts that a wrong answer costs one of, with double XP for finishing with one left and nothing lost for running out; a 90-second speed round on finished skills for fluency, with a best to beat; and short sounds made in the browser (`sounds.ts`), with a switch to turn them off. Dot takes the shape of the topic being practised: a triangle for geometry, a slice of pie for fractions, a diamond for algebra, and so on.

Progress is kept only in the browser's local storage (`progress.ts`): one versioned object, changed by pure functions and checked whenever it is read back, so anything unreadable starts afresh. A test makes every question of every level's lesson for every skill in the course.

Variety: when it makes a question, the app tries a few and prefers a form (`formOf` in `skills.ts`: the kind of question and its wording, without the numbers) that the pupil has not seen in this lesson or the last few on that skill, which progress remembers. A question answered wrongly comes back in the same form, so the pupil can put right what they just learnt. The generators supply the forms: every skill on the path has several reasoning questions with different right answers (see `packages/generators/src/shared/variety.ts`).

Run it locally with `pnpm learn` (http://localhost:5175). `vite build --mode single` inlines the fonts, for hosts that serve only the page and its scripts.

It is online at https://learnmaths.jedhunt.co.uk: `.github/workflows/learn.yml` builds it on every push to `main` and publishes it through the `huntj/learnmaths` repository. The one-off set-up is in [docs/deploy-learn-maths.md](../../docs/deploy-learn-maths.md).

Teacher mode (a switch on the You page, or "I’m a teacher" on the first screen) opens every skill and review on the path (`open` in `buildPath`) and puts a Try button on each level of a skill's sheet. A lesson at a level picked this way is marked `picked`, and finishing it counts that level as reached rather than moving up one.

Unit lessons (`packages/learning/src/unit-lessons/`, one file per unit, played by `UnitLessonView.tsx`) open a unit with its big idea, Brilliant-style: a lesson is a list of screens, each one of a few reusable kinds (`types.ts`). For graphs: **explain**, a function **machine** whose outputs the pupil predicts, **plot** points by tapping a grid, a **choice** whose picture can change once answered, a **slider** for m or c until the line hits a star, **build** an equation from chips, and **drag-line**, two dots to drag until the line is right. For everything else (`unit-screens.tsx`): type an **answer** (with nudges for common wrong ones), **place** numbers on a number line, put cards in **order**, **sort** cards into groups, **fill** gaps from chips, **shade** squares of a grid, solve an equation on a **balance** by doing the same to both sides (with the letter on one side or both), work something out a step at a time (**steps**), turn a line to make an angle (**dial**, with or without a protractor), **spin** a spinner many times, grow a factor **tree**, and **morph** a shape with a slider to see why its area or volume formula works (a circle's slices laid out in a row among them). A **steps** screen's picture can change with each step, as in a compass construction. Any of them can show a picture (`Picture.tsx`): a coordinate grid, a number line, angles round a point, a labelled shape, bar models, a grid, a cuboid, a spinner, a table, a growing pattern, a ruler, big maths, a place value chart, a bar or vertical line chart, a pie chart, or a free drawing of lines, compass arcs and labels (with angles marked by an arc, and side labels kept clear of their side). A coordinate grid can also show shapes and upright lines, for transformations, and quadratic and inverse curves. A number line can show an inequality running off one end; a grid can carry labels and a value in each square, as in a sample space, and a shade screen can ask for exactly the right squares; there are Venn diagrams and dot patterns for triangular and square numbers, and gap fills can accept their answers in any order. The grid (`LineGraph.tsx`) snaps taps to whole-number points and works by keyboard (arrow keys and Enter), as do the number line and the dial. A unit can have one lesson, a stop at the top of the unit on the path, or be split into several (an array in its file, each with an `id`), each a stop just before the skill it teaches (`before`). A split lesson follows one plan, its screens tagged with their part: **The problem** (a real one it can't yet solve), **Warm-up**, **Discover**, a **worked** example revealed a step at a time, **Together**, **Your turn**, **Watch out** (a common mistake put to the pupil as a question), **Spot the mistake** (tap the wrong line of someone's working), **label** the sides of a shape (pick a label such as H, O or A, then tap its side), **Back to the problem**, and an **Exit check**: a **quiz** with no hints, where a wrong answer comes back at the end with new numbers (each question has its `retry`). Finishing a lesson gives 10 XP, counts towards the day's goal, and is kept in `unitLessons` by lesson id; the skills it teaches (`teaches`, else its `before` skill, else the whole unit) then skip their worked examples. A new lesson shows only in teacher mode; once reviewed, its unit goes in `OPEN_TO_PUPILS` in `unit-lessons/index.ts`. Labels on shapes (`labels.ts`) stay level and sit just clear of their side, however it slopes; each labelled angle gets an arc, with its label just past it and clear of both arms. `unit-lessons.test.ts` checks every screen can be done: targets on the line or grid, one right option, the right chips on offer, a balance that ends on a whole number, a factor tree's primes, and words whose maths renders; and that a unit's lessons come in path order, and a split lesson opens on its problem, comes back to it, and ends on an exit check; and that no label on any shape crosses a side or another label.
