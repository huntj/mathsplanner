# @mathsplanner/lesson-player

The screens that play a lesson, in React. They were moved out of `apps/learn` so that Learn Maths and Maths City (see the [proposal](../../docs/proposals/city-game.md)) play the same lessons from one copy. The lessons themselves, and what happens to a pupil's progress when one is finished, are in [`@mathsplanner/learning`](../learning/README.md).

- `LessonView.tsx`: a lesson of generated questions, with Check, the feedback panel and the end screen.
- `UnitLessonView.tsx`, `unit-screens.tsx`, `unit-ui.tsx`: a unit lesson and each kind of screen it can show.
- `variation-screens.tsx`: the learning stage with variation ([proposal](../../docs/proposals/lesson-variation.md)). A **pair** shows a worked example with a question just like it underneath. A **set** asks short questions one at a time, each able to say what changed from the one before and to ask for a prediction first, which is checked but never marked wrong. A lesson with a `method` gets a Method button once its method card has been shown, which brings back the method and the worked example (not in the exit check).
- `unit-ui.tsx` and `speech.ts`: few words ([proposal](../../docs/proposals/lesson-interactive.md#4-few-words-and-explainers-in-a-bubble)). A line of working shows its maths, the written `reason` in brackets (worded from `REASON` in `unit-lessons/reasons.ts`), and any `note` in **Dot's bubble** beside it. Explain screens can carry a bubble too. Hints and feedback come from Dot in a bubble in the answer bar. **Read aloud** (on hold since 21 November 2026: device voices sounded too robotic; turn it back on with `READ_ALOUD` in `speech.ts`), in the lesson bar, speaks the screen's question, options and Dot's words with the maths in words ("18 over 25", "x squared"), using only a voice on the device; with none, the button isn't shown.
- `calc-screen.tsx`: **calc**, working with a box on each line, typed a line at a time as in an exercise book, with each done line's written reason and Dot's hint after a wrong try. A summary screen's `book` shows on a ruled card, "Write this in your book". Both belong to the stepped plan ([template](../../docs/proposals/lesson-stepped.md)).
- **Spot the mistake** shows someone else's working on exercise-book paper, with their name, for the pupil to tap the wrong line. A prediction in a question set shows the diagram before the change beside the one now.
- `interactive-screens.tsx`: hands-on screens ([proposal](../../docs/proposals/lesson-interactive.md)). **Tap** (and a **tap round**, `tapset`: the same question on several diagrams in turn, any of several right sets accepted) asks the pupil to tap parts of a diagram (lines, angles or points carrying a `tap` name), with a nudge for each wrong part and the answer shown after two wrong goes. **Explore** comes before the method: the pupil predicts, then drags points on a geometry diagram (round a circle, along a ray, as a tangent point, or freely), shades a hundred square or changes a total to share, watching live readouts of angles, sums, lengths and ratios. Once they have tried four positions it asks what they noticed. Points drag by pointer or arrow keys, and the diagram's frame stays fixed while they move.
- `Picture.tsx`, `LineGraph.tsx`, `labels.ts`: the pictures a screen can show, and where their labels go.
- `Dot.tsx`, `Icons.tsx`, `BadgeMark.tsx`, `sounds.ts`: Dot and the logo, the icons, the badge marks and the sounds.
- `fit.ts`: shrinks a piece of maths that is too wide for its line. An app calls `watchMaths` once on its root.
- `unit-lessons.test.ts`: checks that every screen of every unit lesson can be done.

Import `@mathsplanner/lesson-player/lesson.css` after the app's own stylesheet. It styles only the lesson screens. It takes its colours from the app's CSS variables and builds on the app's base styles for buttons and topic families, which each app sets for itself, as with `@mathsplanner/ui/widgets.css`.

Like `@mathsplanner/learning`, the package is used from its TypeScript source and has no build of its own.
